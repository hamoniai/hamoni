= Design principles
:navtitle: Design principles

This page describes the design principles used across Hamoni.

== Human-centred design at Hamoni

Hamoni applies human-centred design by starting with the work people do, not
with the software that supports it. The design process begins with the people,
the decisions and the hand-offs that already exist in an organisation.

The product is designed for business people, not technologists. The common path
is configured by the team itself, without specialist help. Prototypes use real
content and real states, and they are validated with the people who do the work.
Accessibility and localisation are part of the design rather than a later
addition.

People remain in control of the work. AI participates in the process, and a
change proposed by AI or by a prompt is reviewable before it is applied.

== Key design principles

=== The 80/20 rule

Eighty percent of things are easy. The common path is configured by the team
itself and does not require specialist help.

The remaining twenty percent is advanced mode. Advanced mode is one
application-wide setting that reveals the technical surface wherever it applies.
There is no per-screen advanced switch.

=== Every action has an actor

Every action in Hamoni is performed by an actor. An actor is a person, a system
account or an AI agent.

A system or agent has its own account. It does not act through a person's
account. The account is managed like any other actor: it can be assigned to
teams and its actions are attributed to it.

The remaining principles apply across the product:

* **Easy by default, advanced on request.** The common path is simple; the
  technical surface is available when the person asks for it.
* **Guiding.** The interface states the next action and the outcome.
* **One design language.** Controls, copy and visual treatment are consistent
  across every surface.
* **Trust through visibility.** Changes are reviewable, auditable and
  attributable to the actor who authorised them.
* **Accessible and localisable.** Every control is operable, every state is
  visible, and every user-facing string is localisable.

== Tone of voice

Copy is plain, direct and written for business people. It states what is
happening and what to do next. It is professional, guiding and supportive, and
it does not scold the person.

User-facing copy does not use acronyms or abbreviations. Every user-facing
string is localisable, and the interface does not build a sentence from
fragments.
