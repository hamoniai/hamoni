= Design
:navtitle: Design

Hamoni is designed for the people who do the work. The product is an
Operations-first platform, and most of the people who use it are business
people, not technologists. The design therefore starts with the work, the
decisions and the hand-offs that already happen in an organisation, and then
adds technology where it helps.

== Human-centred design

Hamoni follows a human-centred approach to design:

* **Start with the work, not the software.** The first question is what a
  person is trying to achieve and what is getting in the way, not which screen
  or feature to build.
* **Design for the common path.** Eighty percent of things should be easy. The
  common path is configured by the team itself, without specialist help.
* **Expose complexity only when it is wanted.** The remaining twenty percent is
  advanced mode: one application-wide setting that reveals the technical
  surface wherever it applies. There is no per-screen advanced switch.
* **Keep people in control.** A change proposed by AI or by a prompt is
  reviewable before it is applied. Natural language is input, never execution.
* **Make the next action clear.** Copy is guiding and forward-looking. It names
  the step that resolves something or returns the person to normal work.
* **Design for everyone.** Controls are keyboard operable, focus is visible,
  contrast is maintained, images carry meaningful alternative text, and all copy
  is localisable.

The result is a product that brings people and AI together as one workforce,
built to scale, without asking every person to become a workflow specialist.

=== Design process

The design work follows a human-centred cycle:

. **Discover.** Understand the people, the work, the decisions and the
  hand-offs that already exist.
. **Frame.** Describe the problem and the next action in the person's language,
  not in the language of the implementation.
. **Prototype.** Draw the flow and the surface early, with real content and real
  states rather than placeholders.
. **Validate.** Test the prototype with the people who do the work, and change
  the design when the evidence says it is wrong.

The design language and the component reference are the shared artefacts that
keep that work consistent from screen to screen.

== One design language

Hamoni binds one design system across the product. Colours, type, spacing,
radii, focus states, status treatment and component behaviour come from the
*Design System for Hamoni*, not from individual screens. PrimeNG is driven by
the generated Hamoni preset and Tailwind is the layout layer, so the component
library and the application shell cannot drift apart.

The design language is maintained separately:

* link:https://github.com/hamoniai/hamoni-design-system[Design System for Hamoni]

The implementation rules are recorded in

for status against a target.

== The team is the primary concept

The **team** is the primary concept in Hamoni. It is where accountability,
membership and the work itself meet.

A team answers *who is accountable for this work and who does it*. A person
belongs to one or more teams. A team owns processes, and the people in it carry
the work through. Teams are deliberately shallow: a team owns a process, and
there is no nesting beyond one level.

The team shapes the product in several ways:

* **Work is organised around teams.** Inbox and Activity show a person's work
  and the work available to their teams.
* **Ownership is a team responsibility.** The owning team is an accountability
  label, not a permission. Permission is resolved separately from the security
  model.
* **Membership is the normal route to sight.** A person who belongs to the team
  that owns a process can see it. Starting the process is a separate grant.
* **Roles are derived, not assigned.** Team manager, process owner and member
  are derived at sign-in and are additive. A person holds the union of the roles
  they qualify for.
* **Projects do not contain teams.** A project packages processes for
  deployment. It may package work owned by several teams, and a team may own
  processes in several projects. Teams and projects are peers, not a hierarchy.

This is why the product treats the team as the first-class working unit while
the organisation remains the security and isolation boundary.

== How work ships

A **project** is the deployment unit. It packages at least one process, the
business rules those processes share, an environment and a version. A project may
package processes owned by several teams, and a team may own processes in more
than one project.

Publishing creates an immutable version and increments the number. Versions are
linear: there is no branching and no reversion. Going back is a redeploy —
moving an environment's pointer to an older version — not a new version that
copies an old one.

An environment is a label on a project, and there is one by default. An
environment points at a version, so promoting a build moves a pointer rather than
copying anything.

Assignment and delivery are separate. Assignment — whose work this is — is set on
the task per environment, shared by default. Delivery — whether it reaches a real
queue — is gated by whether the deployment is classified as production. This
keeps a test environment from routing test work to the production team.

== Design principles

The design principles that follow from this are:

* **People-powered and people-led.** The product is built around people and the
  work they do, with AI as a contributing participant.
* **Easy by default, advanced on request.** The common path is simple; the
  technical surface is available when the person asks for it.
* **One language, one system.** Copy, controls and visual treatment are
  consistent across every surface.
* **Guiding, not scolding.** The interface points to the next action and
  supports the person, even when something has gone wrong.
* **Trust through visibility.** Changes are reviewable, auditable and
  attributable to the person who authorised them.
* **Accessible and localisable by construction.** Accessibility and translation
  are part of the design, not a later retrofit.
